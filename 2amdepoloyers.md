# The 2am Shoppers  
  
  
**An AI shopping companion that remembers your interests, follows your budget and adapts when you respond.**  
  
  
**Hackathon:** Problem Statement 1 by Eslitec    
**Focus:** Personalised, automated e-commerce conversations that support retention and repeat purchases    
**Target audience:** Working youths, approximately 18–30    
**Status:** Proposed app concept; marketplace integrations require validation  
  
  
## 1. The Idea in Simple Terms  
  
  
The 2am Shoppers brings shopping information into one place and helps users decide what to buy, when to revisit an item and when to reorder something they already like.  
  
  
With permission, the app uses available purchase records, saved product links, searches inside the app and customer preferences. It sends casual push notifications that start a useful conversation rather than sending the same promotion to everyone.  
  
  
Users can respond, and the AI changes its recommendations, timing and follow-ups accordingly.  
  
  
**Example:** You save office shoes, set a budget under $40 and ask for a payday reminder. The app reminds you at your chosen time, checks available product information and suggests suitable options. If you say the style is wrong, it changes its suggestions.  
  
  
## 2. The Problem We Address  
  
  
Working youths may shop across several platforms, save products they later forget, repeatedly search for the same items and struggle to keep track of useful offers.  
  
  
Meanwhile, businesses already hold customer records and purchase history but may struggle to turn that information into relevant conversations. Generic promotions can miss the customer's actual needs, budget or readiness to buy.  
  
  
The 2am Shoppers connects these needs: customers receive useful shopping assistance, while participating businesses can provide personalised follow-ups and measure whether customers return and purchase again.

## 4. What the App Will Do  
  
  
### Step 1: Learn Your Preferences  
  
  
During onboarding, users choose:  
  
  
- Interests, such as fashion, coffee, beauty or workday essentials.  
- A general budget or a budget for a particular product.  
- Favourite shops and product preferences.  
- Notification frequency and quiet hours.  
- An optional reminder date, such as payday.  
  
  
Preferences can be changed at any time. The app should not infer someone's salary or payday from their age or shopping habits.  
  
  
### Step 2: Bring Shopping Information Together  
  
  
Possible inputs include:  
  
  
- Purchase records from supported, authorised connections.  
- Uploaded receipts or supported order exports.  
- Product links shared by the user.  
- Wishlists and searches within The 2am Shoppers.  
- Existing customer records supplied by a participating merchant through an authorised integration.  
  
  
The app cannot automatically read every marketplace's private search history. External search activity is used only when a supported integration makes it available with appropriate permission.  
  
  
### Step 3: Decide What Might Be Useful Next  
  
  
The app looks for relevant signals, such as:  
  
  
- A saved item the user wants to revisit.  
- Repeated interest in a category within the app.  
- A verified offer matching a chosen budget.  
- A recurring purchase that may need replenishment.  
- Feedback showing that the previous purchase did not suit the customer.  
  
  
It distinguishes confirmed preferences from estimates. For example, previous coffee purchases can suggest a replenishment check, but the customer confirms whether they are actually running low.  
  
  
### Step 4: Send a Casual Push Notification  
  
  
Illustrative notifications:  
  
  
> Still thinking about that bag? 👀 It's now within your $30 budget.  
  
  
> Coffee check ☕ Running low, or still stocked?  
  
  
> You asked for a payday reminder—want to revisit your wishlist?  
  
  
> Buying snacks with your colleagues? There's an eligible group bundle to check out.  
  
  
Price, stock, discount and group-deal claims must come from verified product or merchant information. If current information is unavailable, the app asks the user to check the listing instead of presenting an unverified offer.  
  
  
### Step 5: Adapt to Your Response  
  
  
| Response | What the app does |  
| --- | --- |  
| Interested | Show details, suitable options or the purchase link |  
| Too expensive | Offer eligible lower-cost alternatives or a user-chosen price alert |  
| Remind me later | Ask when and pause the current follow-up |  
| Wrong style | Update preferences and suggest different options |  
| Already bought | End that purchase prompt and offer optional after-purchase help |  
| Still have enough | Pause the replenishment journey |  
| Not interested | Stop this recommendation and adjust future suggestions |  
| Stop notifications | Honour the notification preference and end push follow-ups |  
  
  
Users can also open a chat and explain their needs in their own words.  
  
  
### Step 6: Help You Take the Next Step  
  
  
The app can:  
  
  
- Show relevant product alternatives from available catalogue data.  
- Explain known product differences.  
- Show verified offers and merchant-supported group deals.  
- Open the correct product or reorder link.  
- Prepare a cart where a supported integration permits it.  
  
  
Users review the details and complete the purchase. The initial app does not buy automatically.  
  
  
### Step 7: Follow Up After the Purchase  
  
  
The app checks whether the product suited the customer, directs unresolved problems to support and offers relevant future assistance.  
  
  
For repeat-use products, it can later ask whether replenishment would be useful. A complaint pauses sales suggestions until the issue is addressed.  
  
  
## 5. Personalised Discovery Feed and Marketing  
  
  
The app includes its own TikTok-inspired discovery feed with short product demonstrations, recommendation cards and clear sponsored labels where applicable.  
  
  
Content changes according to the customer's needs:  
  
  
| Customer context | Suggested content |  
| --- | --- |  
| Unsure how a product works | A product demonstration |  
| Choosing between variants | A factual comparison |  
| Concerned about price | A suitable lower-cost option or verified bundle |  
| Enjoyed a previous purchase | A relevant reorder or complementary item |  
| Had a poor experience | Support or product guidance before another sales offer |  
  
  
For external platforms, the app can prepare campaign suggestions and content for merchant review. Actual ad publishing requires supported account access, approved spending and the platform's available campaign controls.  
  
  
**The app controls its own feed. It cannot guarantee that a product will appear in a particular person's real TikTok feed, or assume that it can link a Shopee search to that person's TikTok account.**

## 7. Two App Views  
  
  
### Customer View  
  
  
- Preferences and budget settings.  
- Saved products and reminders.  
- Personalised discovery feed.  
- AI shopping conversation.  
- Available purchase history and reorder links.  
- Notification and data controls.  
  
  
### Business View  
  
  
- Existing customer records and authorised purchase history.  
- Customer segments and active journeys.  
- Reasons for proposed engagement.  
- Conversation outcomes and support handovers.  
- Campaign drafts and approval controls.  
- Repeat-purchase and retention reporting.  
  
  
The business view helps participating merchants engage their own customers. Customer records should remain scoped to authorised merchants; unrelated sellers do not receive another shop's private customer data.  
  
  
### The Business Process Behind the Customer Experience  
  
  
1. **Merchant onboarding:** Choose the goal, such as increasing second purchases within 60 days. Import existing customer records, order history and a current product catalogue. Set permitted offers, margins, contact limits and support rules.  
2. **Check data quality:** Identify missing dates, duplicate orders and unavailable consent information. Match customer records only using supported, authorised identifiers; do not assume accounts on different marketplaces belong to the same person.  
3. **Segment customers:** Assign a relevant journey using actual records and stated preferences. Customers can change segment when new information arrives.  
4. **Select a trigger:** Examples include a delivered first order, a user-requested reminder date, an estimated replenishment point or inactivity beyond a merchant-defined interval.  
5. **Choose the next useful conversation:** Consider purchase history, current catalogue information, customer intent and contact preferences. Explain the choice on the business dashboard.  
6. **Start the approved journey:** Send an authorised message or notification through an implemented channel. Paid advertising remains a separate merchant-approved campaign action.  
7. **Adapt and act:** Update the journey after the customer replies, asks for a later reminder or confirms a purchase. Recheck stock and price before presenting an offer.  
8. **Hand over when needed:** Escalate unresolved complaints, refund requests, uncertain answers or explicit requests for a person. Give the staff member the relevant conversation context and pause sales prompts.  
9. **Exit or follow up:** End after purchase, opt-out or a contact limit. Resume only for a suitable new trigger within the customer's preferences.  
10. **Measure outcomes:** Connect confirmed orders to eligible customer journeys and report results against a holdout group in a real pilot.  
  
  
### Initial Customer Segments  
  
  
| Segment | Evidence | Journey |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| First-time customer | One delivered order | Product guidance and satisfaction check, followed by a suitable second-purchase opportunity |  
| Returning customer | Multiple previous orders | Relevant reordering or complementary products |  
| Possible replenishment customer | Repeat-use product and observed purchase intervals | Ask whether more is needed before offering a reorder |  
| Interested existing customer | Previous customer with a saved item or authorised recent interest | Clarify budget, preferences or hesitation |  
| Inactive customer | No new order beyond the merchant's chosen interval | Ask whether needs changed and offer relevant assistance |  
| Customer with unresolved issue | Open complaint or poor feedback | Support handover; pause promotional follow-ups |  
| Customer who opted out | Recorded contact preference | Exclude from the affected outreach |  
  
  
Customer budget and reminder timing are preferences layered onto these segments, not assumptions about income.  
  
  
## 8. Example End-to-End Journey  
  
  
**Customer:** Alicia, a young working adult.    
**Merchant:** A fictional coffee and workday-snack shop.  
  
  
1. Alicia has previously bought coffee from the shop and agreed to relevant updates.  
2. She searches for office coffee inside The 2am Shoppers.  
3. The app shows a suitable product demonstration in its discovery feed.  
4. Alicia responds: **"Interested, but too expensive."**  
5. The agent checks the merchant's catalogue and offers a smaller pack or an eligible colleague bundle.  
6. Alicia chooses: **"Remind me on the 25th."**  
7. The agent schedules the reminder and pauses immediate follow-ups.  
8. Before the reminder, it checks whether the product and offer are still available.  
9. If something changed, it explains the change and offers suitable alternatives.  
10. Alicia opens the listing, reviews the purchase and completes checkout.  
11. The app records a verified purchase when confirmation is available, ends the current purchase prompt and later checks satisfaction.  
  
  
This journey has a clear trigger, decisions, adaptations and exits. A product-link click alone is not treated as a completed purchase.

## 10. How We Measure Success  
  
  
| Measure | What it tells us |  
| --- | --- |  
| Repeat-purchase rate | Share of eligible existing customers who buy again within a defined period |  
| Retention | Share of a customer cohort who remain active purchasers within a defined period |  
| Time to next purchase | Whether customers return sooner after useful engagement |  
| Customer lifetime value | Longer-term customer contribution; estimates must state their assumptions |  
| Conversation-to-purchase rate | Share of engaged customers with a confirmed purchase within a stated attribution window |  
| Opt-outs and complaints | Whether engagement is becoming unwanted |  
| Support outcomes | Whether customer problems are successfully resolved |  
  
  
For a real pilot, compare eligible customers receiving the journey with a randomly assigned holdout group. Track repeat purchases from the participating business, because purchases elsewhere do not establish retention for that merchant.  
  
  
Cross-platform information can improve relevance where authorised, but a recommendation to another seller is not counted as retention for the original merchant. New-customer ad conversions are reported separately from existing-customer repeat purchases.  
  
  
Hackathon sample data can demonstrate the reporting process. It does not prove a real increase in retention or customer lifetime value.
